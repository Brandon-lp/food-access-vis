07 Take action

You can
take action.

Food access is not one problem, so there is no single fix. These three organizations each work on a different part of it, and each one gives you a way to help.

  1. 01 · Food banks

    Feeding America

    The largest hunger-relief network in the U.S., made up of about 200 food banks. Those food banks supply tens of thousands of local pantries and meal programs, the reach our food bank chart shows stretched thin in many states.

    Take action: Find your local food bank, then volunteer, donate, or share it with someone who needs it. Find a food bank ↗

  2. 02 · Research and policy

    Food Research & Action Center

    A national nonprofit that researches hunger and pushes for stronger programs like SNAP, school meals and WIC. As our SNAP chart shows, benefits haven't kept up with food costs, and policy is where that changes.

    Take action: Sign up for FRAC's updates and tell your representatives to protect and strengthen SNAP and school meals. Visit FRAC ↗

  3. 03 · Meals and pantries

    The Salvation Army

    Runs food pantries and community meals in neighborhoods across the country, and brings hot food to people after disasters. It reaches people close to home, where store distance and tight budgets hit hardest.

    Take action: Volunteer or donate at your local Salvation Army, or help someone find the food pantry nearest them. Find your local Salvation Army ↗
