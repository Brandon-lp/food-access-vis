02 Dimensions

There is more than one kind of access.

Click on each one to explore them.

Physical access

Can you get to a store? The USDA counts people as having low access when they live more than 1 mile from a supermarket in a city, or more than 10 miles in the countryside. In 2019, 68.6 million people lived that far away. Without a car, that means long walks, bus rides or paying for a ride.

Race

Food hardship is not spread evenly. In 2024, 23.6% of Black households and 21.9% of American Indian and Alaska Native households received SNAP food benefits, compared with 8.3% of White households. Where people live and how much they earn shape who can reach, and afford, healthy food.

Economic access

Even when a store is close, food has to fit the budget. In 2024, households receiving SNAP had a median income of $31,207, about a third of the $89,487 for other households. When money is tight, groceries compete with rent, bills and transportation.
